Successful Transmission of 128-QAM SC-FDM over Optical Wireless Communications

Demonstrating high spectral efficiency and low peak-to-average power ratio through FPGA real-time DSP pipelines.

📅 Published: 2024⏱️ 2 min read📍 BONLAB Testbed · RMUTI
Share this story:

The Broadband Optical Network Laboratory (BON LAB) at RMUTI Khon Kaen Campus has achieved a key milestone in optical wireless communications (OWC), successfully demonstrating the transmission and real-time reception of 128-QAM Single-Carrier Frequency Division Multiplexing (SC-FDM) signals.

Under the leadership of Assoc. Prof. Dr. Kidsanapong Puntsri, the research team designed an end-to-end transceiver architecture combining DFT precoding, discrete constellation mapping, and carrier synchronization implemented on high-speed FPGA hardware boards.

Why 128-QAM SC-FDM Matters

In optical wireless communications, transmitter devices such as laser diodes (LDs) and LEDs exhibit nonlinear characteristics that severely degrade multicarrier signals with high Peak-to-Average Power Ratios (PAPR). SC-FDM effectively mitigates this issue while allowing high constellation densities (128-QAM), offering both energy efficiency and high throughput.

Laboratory Validation Setup

The validation setup at BONLAB utilizes arbitrary waveform generators (AWG), optical emitter modules, high-sensitivity photodetectors, and real-time digital oscilloscopes for EVM and BER verification.
